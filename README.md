# Online Media Consumption during COVID-19

### **The Project**
This study examines the structural and psychological impact of the 2020 lockdowns on human digital behavior. Moving beyond descriptive statistics, the project utilizes inferential modelling to identify the thresholds where digital utility transforms into a psychological burden.

### **The Core Insight: The "Consumption Paradox"**
While usage hours spiked across all demographics, the data revealed a counter-intuitive **negative correlation ($r = -0.46$)** between high usage and misinformation vulnerability. As digital platforms became essential for education and remote work, users effectively developed higher digital resilience and stronger "filtering" capabilities.

### **Key Findings**
* **Systemic Migration:** Validated a permanent shift in daily habits, with a significant majority moving to high-frequency usage (3+ hours)
* **Psychological Thresholds:** Identified the specific tipping point where digital connectivity transitions from a survival tool into a measurable mental health strain
* **Utility vs. Exhaustion:** Mapped the relationship between perceived online helpfulness and social discomfort post-lockdown.
